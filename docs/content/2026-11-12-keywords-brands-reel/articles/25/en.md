:::summary
The main **types of cat litter** are clumping clay (bentonite), clay with activated carbon for odor control, silica crystals and plant-based litters made from wood, corn, tofu or paper. Most cats prefer fine, soft, unscented litter, so the best litter is simply the one your cat uses without fuss. For kittens that taste their litter, non-clumping paper or pellets are safer. Scoop every day and replace the litter completely on a regular schedule.
:::

The litter aisle can be confusing: clumping, carbon, crystal, tofu, wood, and every bag promises "odor-free" and "dust-free". Your cat doesn't read the bag, though; it cares how the litter feels underfoot, how it smells and how clean the box is. This guide compares the types of cat litter on clumping, dust, smell, weight, disposal and kitten safety, explains how often to change it, and shows when a toilet problem has nothing to do with the litter.

## What types of cat litter are there?

### Clumping clay (bentonite)

Bentonite is a clay that swells when wet and forms firm clumps you can scoop out. It's the most common litter worldwide.

- **Pros:** the fine, sandy texture suits most cats, and because urine comes out with each clump, odor stays low.
- **Cons:** it's heavy to carry, cheaper versions can be dusty, it sticks to paws and gets tracked around, and it doesn't biodegrade.

Non-clumping clay also exists. It absorbs urine but you can't remove it, so it smells sooner and needs a full change more often.

### What is activated carbon cat litter?

Carbon litter is usually bentonite with black or grey granules of activated carbon mixed in. Activated carbon traps odor molecules on its surface instead of masking them with perfume. It suits small apartments and multi-cat homes, but doesn't replace daily scooping, and its dark dust can mark light floors. If a litter contains both carbon and fragrance, choose the unscented version.

### Silica crystals

Silica gel crystals soak up urine moisture and lock in odor. They're light, very low in dust and last longer between changes. On the other hand they don't clump, so you remove stool daily and stir the crystals. Some cats dislike the hard grains and the crunching sound, and crystals aren't suitable for kittens that taste their litter.

### Plant-based litters: wood, corn, tofu and paper

- **Wood pellets:** compressed sawdust that crumbles when wet; non-clumping, low in dust, with a mild wood scent. Some cats dislike the large pellets.
- **Corn and wheat:** clumping and light, but keep them dry, because damp grain litter can go mouldy.
- **Tofu:** made from soybean fibre; clumps well and is very low in dust.
- **Recycled paper:** soft, nearly dust-free and non-clumping; good for kittens, and vets sometimes recommend it after surgery.

Plant-based litters are lighter and biodegradable, though odor control varies by type. Even if the bag says "flushable", don't flush it: it can block pipes and carries parasites from cat feces into the water system.

### Quick comparison

- **Clumping:** bentonite, tofu and corn yes; silica, wood pellets and paper no.
- **Dust:** paper, tofu and silica lowest; bentonite depends on quality.
- **Odor control:** carbon and silica strongest, but no litter replaces daily scooping.
- **Weight:** bentonite heaviest; silica and plant-based lighter.
- **Disposal:** plant-based litters biodegrade; clay and silica don't.

## Which cat litter is best for your situation?

No litter is best for every cat, but most cats prefer fine, soft and unscented. Start here:

- **Kittens:** paper or non-clumping pellets until they stop tasting litter, then switch gradually to clumping if you like.
- **Senior cats or stiff joints:** soft, fine litter in a box with a low entry.
- **Long-haired cats:** very fine litter sticks to paw and tail fur; larger grains stick less.
- **Asthma or allergies at home:** low-dust, unscented litter such as paper, tofu or silica.
- **Lots of stairs:** silica or a light plant-based litter.
- **Several cats:** a clumping litter with good odor control, such as carbon, plus extra boxes.

Not sure? Offer two boxes with two litters for a week and see which your cat chooses, and mix any new type into the old over a week or two. ## Is there a truly dust-free, odor-free cat litter?

No litter is completely dust-free, but low-dust types make a real difference. Pour slowly, close to the bottom of the box.

As for smell, remember that a cat's nose is far more sensitive than ours. Scented litter and air fresheners near the box may please you but drive your cat away. Bad odor comes from what's left in the box, not from the litter type:

- Scoop twice a day in hot weather.
- Put the box somewhere quiet but with some airflow.
- Replace a plastic box once it's heavily scratched, roughly once a year; scratches hold odor.

## How often should you change cat litter?

Scoop clumps and stool once or twice a day and top up so the litter stays about 5 to 7 cm deep. How often you replace it completely depends on the type:

- **Clumping (bentonite, tofu, corn):** roughly every 2 to 4 weeks.
- **Non-clumping (paper, wood pellets, plain clay):** once or twice a week; remove pellet sawdust daily.
- **Silica:** follow the bag, usually when the crystals are saturated and odor returns.

For a full change:

1. Put on gloves. If you're pregnant, ask someone else to do it.
2. Tip all the litter into a bin bag, tie it and put it in the household waste.
3. Wash the box with warm water and mild, unscented soap. Skip bleach and strong disinfectants; some are toxic to cats.
4. Dry the box thoroughly so fresh litter doesn't stick to the bottom.
5. Add 5 to 7 cm of fresh litter and put the box back in its usual spot.
6. Wash your hands.

[[INFOGRAPHIC]]

Daily scooping isn't only about smell: Toxoplasma eggs in fresh feces take one to five days to become infectious, so a box cleaned every day is safer for the whole household.

## How many litter boxes do you need, and where?

The rule of thumb is one box per cat, plus one. Two boxes side by side count as one, so put them in different places. Each box should be at least one and a half times your cat's body length, in a quiet, always-accessible spot away from food and water. Many cats prefer open boxes to covered ones. ## When is a litter box problem medical?

New litter or a dirty box can put a cat off, but some signs have nothing to do with litter and need a vet:

- Straining in the box with no urine or only a few drops.
- Frequent trips with small amounts, or crying while peeing.
- Blood in the urine or pink clumps; easier to see on light or crystal litter.
- Peeing or pooping outside the box, especially if it starts suddenly.
- Clumps much bigger or more frequent than usual, which can mean your cat is drinking and peeing more.
- Diarrhea, constipation, or an adult cat eating litter.

For a cat peeing outside the box, our guide to [why a cat pees outside the litter box](/en/blog/cat-peeing-outside-litter-box) helps find the cause. Cats on dry food alone often drink little and produce concentrated urine; water and wet food are covered in our guide to the [best dry cat food](/en/blog/best-dry-cat-food).

:::warning
Emergency: a cat, especially a male, that keeps going to the box and straining but produces no urine or only drops may have a blocked urethra. This becomes life-threatening quickly; get to a vet within the hour. Show your vet blood in the urine the same day, and peeing outside the box or any sudden change in toilet habits as soon as you can. [Find a vet near you](/en/vet/find). ## Types of cat litter: frequently asked questions

:::faq
? Which type of cat litter is best?
The one your cat uses without fuss. Most cats prefer fine, soft, clumping, unscented litter; paper or pellets suit kittens.
? How often should I change cat litter?
Scoop once or twice a day. Replace clumping litter fully every 2 to 4 weeks and non-clumping litter once or twice a week; follow the bag for silica.
? What is activated carbon cat litter?
Usually bentonite with activated carbon granules, which trap urine odor instead of covering it with perfume. Choose an unscented version.
? How deep should cat litter be?
About 5 to 7 cm. Too little and clumps stick to the bottom; too much is wasted.
? What is the best litter for a kitten?
Paper or non-clumping pellets while the kitten still tastes its litter; switch to clumping gradually later.
? Why does my cat's litter box smell?
Usually infrequent scooping, too few boxes or an old plastic box. If the urine smell suddenly gets much stronger, ask your vet.
:::

The right cat litter is the one your cat uses happily and you can keep clean every day. Choose by your cat and your home, switch gradually, and take any sudden change in toilet habits seriously.
