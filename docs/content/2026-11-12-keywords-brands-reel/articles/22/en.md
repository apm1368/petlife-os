Hauling a 12 kg sack of dog food up the stairs, or driving around town for your cat's usual food, gets old fast, which is why so many of us now order from an online pet shop. But pet food is also sold as counterfeits, repacked bags and stock close to its date, and a hot delivery van can spoil food that left the warehouse in perfect shape. Here's what to check before you order, on delivery and once the bag is open.

## What does a trustworthy online pet shop show you?

A good shop has nothing to hide. Before your first order from any online pet store, look for:

- **A real seller:** a company name, a physical address and contact details that get answered. On marketplaces, check who the third-party seller is.
- **Photos of the real product,** or a willingness to send one on request.
- **Batch code and best-before date,** listed or confirmed before dispatch.
- **Complete details:** species, life stage, net weight, ingredients and country of manufacture.
- **Written return rules and a secure checkout,** not a bank transfer to a private account.

Warning signs: a price far below everyone else's, orders only via social-media messages with no address or invoice, and vague answers about dates.

## How do you tell genuine pet food from fake or repacked?

The biggest risks when you buy cat or dog food online are counterfeits and repacked food: kibble poured from large sacks into small, unbranded bags. You can't know its source, age or how long it sat open, and the fat in dry food oxidises with air, light and heat and turns rancid. Check for:

- **Intact factory packaging:** an even seal or stitching, with no tape, glue or staples.
- **Clean printing:** no blurry, crooked or misspelled text.
- **Batch code and date printed on the bag itself,** not on a sticker added later. Two different dates is a red flag.
- **Importer details** where your country requires them for imported products.
- **After opening:** the smell you know, not sour or musty, and kibble that is even in colour, dry and free of clumps.

If a pet that always eats this food happily suddenly refuses a new bag, suspect the bag. You can also ask the seller to photograph the batch code on the exact bag they'll send. ## Summer heat, delivery checks and returns

A parcel that spent hours in a hot van or warehouse can look fine but go rancid sooner. In hot weather, choose a cooler delivery slot and don't order when nobody is home to take the box in from the sun.

### What should you check when your order arrives?

1. Before the courier leaves, look for tears, damp patches, grease stains or crushed corners.
2. Film or photograph the unboxing; if anything is wrong, that's your evidence.
3. Compare the batch code and date with what the seller told you.
4. Reject punctured or greasy bags, and never use bulging, rusty or leaking cans.
5. Match product, species, life stage and weight to your order.
6. Report problems in writing the same day and keep the food bag closed until it's sorted.

[[INFOGRAPHIC]]

Opened food is rarely taken back. Before paying, find out how long you have to return damaged or wrong items, who pays for it, and whether a collar or carrier can be swapped for another size. Consumer rights differ between countries, so read the shop's own policy.

## Big bag or small? Opening and storing dry food

A big bag usually costs less per kilo, but once it's open, the freshness clock starts ticking. The simple rule: buy a size your pet finishes in four to six weeks. Take the daily amount for your pet's weight from the feeding table and multiply by 30; a cat eating about 60 g a day gets through nearly 2 kg a month. When you compare bags in the [shop's food section](/en/shop/products?category=food), check the net weight against that number.

Once the bag arrives:

1. Cut it straight across the top so it closes again, and photograph the batch code and date.
2. Keep the food in its original bag, whose lining protects the fat; roll the top down and clip it.
3. Put the whole bag in an airtight container; if you pour food straight in, wash it before each new bag.
4. Store it somewhere cool, dry and dark, not in a hot garage or next to the oven.
5. Cover and refrigerate opened wet food and use it within the time on the label, usually a few days.

If this is a new food, mix it in gradually over 7 to 10 days; a sudden switch is a common cause of vomiting and diarrhoea.

## Is autoship or a pet food subscription worth it?

Repeat delivery is great if you always remember on the last scoop, as long as:

- The interval matches what your pet really eats, so bags don't pile up.
- You check the date on every delivery, not just the first.
- The shop tells you if the product is out of stock instead of sending a substitute.

No subscription? A phone reminder a week before the bag runs out works too, and a small spare bag means a late parcel never forces a sudden food change.

## Buying pet supplies online: size and material matter

With supplies, the usual problem is fit and quality rather than fakes:

- **Collars and harnesses:** measure neck and chest and compare with that product's size chart.
- **Carriers:** your pet should be able to stand, turn and lie down, with a secure door and good airflow.
- **Litter boxes:** at least one and a half times your cat's body length.
- **Toys:** no small parts or loose strings, and too big to swallow.
- **Bowls:** stainless steel or glazed ceramic are more hygienic than thin plastic.

Cat litter is heavy, so ordering it online saves your back, but don't switch types on impulse; cats are fussy about texture and smell. Compare the options in our guide to [types of cat litter](/en/blog/cat-litter-types).

## When to buy from your vet or a local shop instead

Some purchases belong elsewhere:

- **Prescription diets** (urinary, kidney, digestive or hypoallergenic): start or stop them only on your vet's advice; fed to a healthy pet without reason, they can do harm.
- **Medicines and parasite treatments:** the right product and dose depend on weight, age and health, so choose them with your vet.
- **Urgent needs:** if you've run out or your pet is unwell, don't wait for a delivery.

Thinking about cooking for your dog instead? Read our guide to [homemade dog food](/en/blog/homemade-dog-food) first; home-cooked meals without a nutritionist's recipe are easily unbalanced.

:::warning
Don't feed it, and contact the seller, if you find a sour or musty smell, damp or clumped kibble, insects, bulging or leaking cans, or a date that has passed or been tampered with. If your pet develops repeated vomiting or diarrhoea, marked lethargy or loss of appetite after starting a new bag, stop the food, keep the bag and call your vet the same day.
:::

## Online pet shop: frequently asked questions

:::faq
? How can I tell if pet food bought online is genuine?
Intact factory packaging, clean printing, a batch code and date printed on the bag itself and proper importer details. Ask for a photo of the date on your bag before ordering.
? Is repacked or loose pet food safe?
Not necessarily toxic, but its source and age are unknown and the fat goes rancid faster. If you must, buy small amounts, check the smell and use it quickly.
? What should I check when my order arrives?
Damage to the box and bag, the batch code and date, bulging or leaking cans, and whether the product matches your order. Film the unboxing.
? Should I buy a big or small bag of dry food?
A size your pet finishes in four to six weeks. Big bags suit large dogs or multi-cat homes; for one cat they often go stale.
? Can I return opened pet food?
Usually not, unless it's spoiled, out of date or wrong. Read the policy first and keep the bag, invoice and unboxing video.
? Can I buy prescription pet food online?
Only once your vet has prescribed it; the vet also decides which diet and for how long.
:::
