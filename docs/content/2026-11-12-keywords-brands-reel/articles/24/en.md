## What is the best dry dog food for your dog?

No single food is best for every dog. A food labelled "complete and balanced" is formulated to meet all known nutrient needs for its life stage. Among those foods, the best one for your dog is the one that:

- matches their age, size and activity;
- they eat willingly without vomiting, diarrhoea or itching afterwards;
- keeps them at an ideal weight, with firm stools and a healthy coat;
- comes from a reputable seller in an intact, dated bag.

Labels like "holistic" or "premium" tell you little on their own; the nutrition guidelines of the World Small Animal Veterinary Association ([WSAVA](https://wsava.org/)) focus on the label, the manufacturer and your dog's body condition. ## Choosing dry food by age, size and activity

### Puppies

Puppies need more energy and minerals per kilogram than adults, so choose a food labelled for growth or all life stages. Large-breed puppies, those expected to reach roughly 25 to 30 kg or more as adults, need a food that states it is suitable for the growth of large-size dogs: too much calcium and too many calories during growth can harm bone and joint development. Do not add calcium supplements, and aim for a lean puppy that grows steadily rather than fast. Feed three to four meals a day until about six months. Small breeds usually move to adult food at around 9 to 12 months, large breeds at 12 to 18 months or later; ask your vet when.

### Small breeds such as Pomeranians and Shih Tzus

Small dogs burn more energy per kilogram, but their total daily portion is tiny, so a few table scraps or extra treats quickly add weight. Small-breed formulas have smaller kibble that suits small mouths. Toy-breed puppies can develop low blood sugar if they skip meals, so keep their meals regular.

### Medium, large and very active dogs

An adult with normal activity needs an adult maintenance food; high-energy "performance" foods are for dogs that truly work or run for hours a day. Split large dogs' daily portion into at least two meals.

### Neutered and senior dogs

After neutering, energy needs drop while appetite often rises; without a smaller portion, weight creeps up. There is no official standard for "senior" food and recipes vary widely, so match an older dog's food to regular check-ups and your vet's advice.

### Therapeutic diets only on your vet's advice

Kidney, digestive, liver, hypoallergenic and prescription weight-loss diets are designed for specific conditions. Use one only when your vet recommends it after an examination.

## How to read a dry dog food label

Before you buy, check:

- **The nutritional adequacy statement:** "complete and balanced" (often referencing AAFCO in North America) or "complete feed" (the European wording), plus the life stage; for large-breed puppies, growth of large-size dogs. "Complementary" foods are not a full diet.
- **Ingredients:** listed by weight. A named animal protein near the top is a good sign, but not a guarantee of quality.
- **Guaranteed analysis:** protein, fat, fibre and moisture.
- **Calories:** kcal per kg or per cup, which you need to work out portions.
- **Manufacturer details:** contact information, batch number and best-before date.

## Is grain-free dry dog food better?

Not automatically. Dogs digest cooked grains well, and food allergies are less common than marketing suggests, are not limited to grains and are diagnosed with an elimination diet under a vet's supervision. In recent years the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has looked into reports of a heart muscle disease, dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM), in dogs eating certain grain-free diets high in peas, lentils or other legumes. A cause-and-effect link has not been established and research is ongoing. Talk to your vet before choosing a grain-free diet.

## How much dry food should I feed my dog?

The feeding guide is written for an "average" dog, and yours may not be one. Instead:

1. Weigh your dog: hold a small dog on your bathroom scale and subtract your own weight; weigh a large dog at the vet clinic.
2. Find the daily amount for your dog's ideal weight; if your dog is overweight, agree a target weight with your vet.
3. Weigh the portion on a kitchen scale, because measuring cups are imprecise.
4. Split it into two meals (three to four for puppies).
5. Keep treats, training treats included, under 10% of daily calories and subtract them from the portion.
6. Weigh your dog every two to four weeks. If weight creeps up, cut the portion by about 10%; if it drops, add the same.

For example, a neutered 10 kg adult with normal activity needs about 600 to 650 kcal a day. With a food containing 370 kcal per 100 g, that is roughly 160 to 180 g a day. Treat that as an illustration only. Training a puppy? Use part of the daily kibble as rewards, and see our guide to [puppy training by age](/en/blog/puppy-training-by-age). Avoid table scraps as rewards: some human foods are dangerous, as our list of [toxic foods for dogs](/en/blog/toxic-foods-for-dogs) shows.

### What does an ideal body condition look like?

Vets use a body condition score from 1 to 9, where 4 to 5 is ideal. You should feel the ribs under a thin layer of fat with gentle pressure, see a waist from above and see the belly tuck up slightly from the side. If you struggle to find the ribs, your dog is carrying extra weight.

## Buying and storing a big bag of kibble

Price and country of origin do not decide quality on their own; a complete label, freshness and an intact original bag matter more. Avoid food sold loose by weight with no label. Buying online? Our [online pet shop checklist](/en/blog/online-pet-shop-guide) covers what to check on delivery. To keep kibble fresh:

- match the bag size to your dog: a 15 kg bag can take a small dog many months and go stale;
- finish an opened bag within about four to six weeks;
- keep the food in its original bag, rolled shut, inside a lidded bin;
- store it somewhere cool, dry and out of the sun;
- throw away food that smells rancid or shows mould or insects.

## How to switch dog food safely

A sudden change is a common cause of diarrhoea and wind. Switch over 7 to 10 days:

- days 1 to 3: a quarter new food;
- days 4 to 6: half and half;
- days 7 to 9: three quarters new;
- day 10: new food only.

If stools turn soft, stay at the current step for a day or two. And don't feed dog food to your cat: it lacks enough taurine and other nutrients cats need.

:::warning
See a vet if your dog has vomiting or diarrhoea together with lethargy, vomits repeatedly, has blood in the stool or vomit, or loses weight suddenly. A swollen, hard belly with retching that brings nothing up and restlessness, especially in large, deep-chested dogs after a meal, is an emergency: go to a vet immediately. A dog that has not eaten for more than a day, or a toy-breed puppy that is weak, trembling or listless, also needs to be seen right away.
:::

## Frequently asked questions

:::faq
? How much dry food should I feed my dog per day?
Start with the feeding guide for your dog's ideal weight and weigh the portion. A neutered 10 kg adult often needs about 160 to 180 g of a food with 370 kcal per 100 g, but the weight trend decides.
? Is dry food good for dogs?
Yes. A complete and balanced dry food suits most healthy dogs and is convenient and easy to portion, as long as you control the amount and fresh water is always available.
? What is the best dry food for small breeds?
Any complete and balanced food with small kibble that suits your dog's life stage. With small dogs, controlling portions and treats matters most.
? Should I choose a grain-free dog food?
Only after talking to your vet. Grain-free is not automatically healthier, and possible links between some grain-free diets and heart disease are still being studied.
:::
