## What makes the best dry cat food for your cat?

Cats are obligate carnivores: they need plenty of animal protein and nutrients such as taurine that must come from their diet. Any food labelled "complete and balanced" is formulated to cover those needs. Among those foods, the best one for your cat is the one that:

- matches their life stage and situation;
- they eat willingly, without vomiting or diarrhoea afterwards;
- keeps them at an ideal weight;
- comes from a reputable seller in an intact, clearly dated bag.

Words like "holistic" or "grain-free" say nothing about quality on their own; the nutrition guidelines of the World Small Animal Veterinary Association ([WSAVA](https://wsava.org/)) focus on the label, the manufacturer and your cat's body condition. For background on established manufacturers, see our guide to [the best pet food brands](/en/blog/top-pet-food-and-gear-brands).

## Choosing dry food by life stage and lifestyle

### Kittens

Kittens need more energy, protein and minerals per kilogram than adults, so choose a food labelled for growth or all life stages. When kittens start on solids at around four weeks, soften the kibble with warm water. Most cats stay on kitten food until about 12 months, some large breeds longer. Feed kittens under six months three to four small meals a day. Found an orphaned kitten that still needs milk? Read our guide to [orphan kitten care](/en/blog/orphan-kitten-care); cow's milk is not a substitute.

### Neutered and indoor adults

From one year, an adult or all-life-stages food is appropriate. After neutering, energy needs drop noticeably while appetite often rises, and indoor cats move less, which is why many house cats gain weight. Neutered or indoor formulas usually have fewer calories per 100 g, but portion control and daily play matter more than the name on the bag. Our article on [spaying and neutering cats](/en/blog/cat-spay-neuter-guide) covers what changes after surgery.

### Senior cats

Cats count as senior from about 11 years. There is no official standard for "senior" food, and recipes vary widely. A senior cat that is slowly losing weight needs a vet check, not just a new food.

### Therapeutic diets only on your vet's advice

Urinary, kidney and digestive diets are designed for specific medical conditions and may not suit a healthy cat. Use one only when your vet recommends it after an examination.

## How to read a dry cat food label

Before you buy, check:

- **The nutritional adequacy statement:** "complete and balanced" (often referencing AAFCO in North America) or "complete feed" (the European wording), plus the life stage. "Complementary" foods and treats are not a full diet.
- **Ingredients:** listed by weight. A named animal protein such as chicken or fish near the top is a good sign, but not a guarantee of quality.
- **Guaranteed analysis:** protein, fat, fibre and moisture; dry food has about 10% moisture.
- **Calories:** kcal per kg or per cup, needed to work out portions.
- **Feeding guide:** a starting point, not a final answer.
- **Manufacturer details:** contact information, batch number and best-before date.

## Does price matter? Buying and storing a bag

A higher price does not automatically mean a better food; what matters is the label, freshness and a manufacturer with good quality control. Avoid food sold loose by weight: you cannot check the date or origin, and fats go rancid faster. Buying online? Our [online pet shop checklist](/en/blog/online-pet-shop-guide) explains what to check on delivery. Once a bag is open:

- buy a size your cat finishes within about four to six weeks;
- keep the food in its original bag, rolled shut, inside an airtight container;
- store it somewhere cool, dry and out of the sun;
- throw away food that smells rancid or shows mould or insects.

## How much dry food should I feed my cat?

Your cat's pleading eyes are not a feeding guide. Instead:

1. Weigh your cat: step on the scale alone, then holding your cat, and subtract.
2. Find the daily amount for your cat's ideal weight in the feeding guide; if your cat is overweight, agree a target with your vet.
3. Weigh the portion on a kitchen scale, because measuring cups are imprecise.
4. Split it into at least two meals, or put some into a food puzzle.
5. Count treats as part of the day and keep them under 10% of calories.
6. Weigh your cat every two to four weeks. If weight creeps up, cut the portion by about 10%; if it drops, add the same.

As an example, a neutered indoor adult of about 4 kg typically needs around 200 to 240 kcal a day. With a food containing 380 kcal per 100 g, that is roughly 50 to 65 g a day. Every cat is different, so treat this as an illustration.

### What does an ideal body condition look like?

Vets use a body condition score from 1 to 9, where 5 is ideal. You should feel the ribs under a thin layer of fat without seeing them, and see a waist behind the ribs from above. Weight loss in cats must be slow and supervised by a vet; crash diets are dangerous for cats.

## Water, wet food and switching foods

Cats have a naturally low thirst drive. Dry food contains about 10% water, wet food around 75 to 80%, so a cat on kibble alone has to drink more. Offer several wide, clean bowls away from the food and litter box, refreshed daily. A complete wet meal also raises water intake; reduce the kibble so calories stay the same. If your vet says your cat needs more water, plan the wet-to-dry ratio together.

A sudden food change can cause vomiting, diarrhoea or a hunger strike. Switch over about 10 days:

- days 1 to 3: a quarter new food;
- days 4 to 6: half and half;
- days 7 to 9: three quarters new;
- day 10: new food only.

Go slower with a fussy cat, but never let them go hungry to "make" them accept it. A cat that stops eating for several days, especially an overweight one, risks a serious liver condition called hepatic lipidosis. If vomiting continues, read about [why cats vomit](/en/blog/cat-vomiting-causes).

## Can cats eat dog food, bread or leftovers?

Dog food usually lacks enough taurine, protein, arachidonic acid and vitamin A for a cat. Cats cannot make enough taurine themselves, and a long-term deficiency leads to heart and eye problems. A few stolen kibbles are harmless; dog food as a regular diet is not.

Feeding community cats? A little complete dry food is far better than bread, leftovers or milk: bread does not meet a cat's needs, leftovers may contain onion, garlic or cooked bones, and cow's milk gives many adult cats diarrhoea. Add fresh water in hot weather and clear away what is left.

:::warning
If your cat has not eaten for more than a day, do not wait for them to get used to a new food: call your vet, and act even sooner for kittens and overweight cats. Repeated vomiting or vomiting with lethargy, diarrhoea lasting more than a day or containing blood, sudden weight loss, and drinking or urinating much more than usual all need an examination. Straining in the litter box or going back and forth with little or no urine, especially in a male cat, is an emergency: see a vet the same day, even at night.
:::

## Frequently asked questions

:::faq
? How much dry food should I feed my cat per day?
Start with the feeding guide for your cat's ideal weight and weigh the portion. A neutered 4 kg adult often needs about 50 to 65 g a day, but the weight trend decides.
? Is dry food bad for cats?
No. A complete and balanced dry food suits a healthy cat, as long as portions are controlled and your cat drinks enough. If your cat drinks little, add some wet food.
? What is the best dry food for neutered or indoor cats?
Any complete and balanced food that keeps your cat at an ideal weight with measured portions. Neutered formulas are usually lower in calories but still need weighing.
? Should I mix wet and dry cat food?
You can, and it helps with water intake. Use complete foods and reduce the dry portion so total calories stay the same.
:::
